Manual SMS import · in beta

Paste a bank alert. Review it. Save it.

In Activity, choose Import SMS, then paste or share a single bank or UPI alert. Artham parses it on your device and you confirm the result before anything is saved.

Artham does not request the RECEIVE_SMS or READ_SMS permissions, does not register an incoming-SMS receiver and does not scan your inbox. Only a message you paste or share is processed.

These alerts are made up. Manual import needs no SMS permission.

Only what you share

Nothing is imported unless you paste or share an alert yourself. There is no background listener and no automatic import.

No SMS permissions

Artham does not ask for RECEIVE_SMS or READ_SMS, and it never scans your message inbox. This holds on both Android and iOS.

Matched by last four digits

The suffix in the alert, such as A/c XX1255, finds your saved account. An unknown or ambiguous suffix is never guessed.

You confirm every import

The parsed amount, date, account and category appear in an editable review. Nothing is saved until you confirm.

Private by design

The original SMS text is not kept. Artham saves structured fields only, and recognises merchants and categories on the device.

Duplicates are caught

Local fingerprints stop the same alert being imported twice, and older fingerprints are kept so earlier imports stay protected.

Carry it forward

Opt in to roll unused overall and category balances into the next month. Only positive balances carry over.

Repeat overall or category limits every month, and pause or resume a template any time. Missing months fill in when you open the app. Existing budgets are never overwritten, and no transactions or balances are touched.

Smart, and fully on your device · in beta

It knows where your money goes.

Artham recognises more than 1,000 India-focused merchants from a catalogue stored in the app, ranks where you spend and notices payments that repeat. Nothing leaves your phone, and nothing changes without you.

Merchants and amounts are made-up examples.

1,075 merchants, built in

Swiggy, BigBasket, Uber, Netflix and more are recognised in your descriptions and bank alerts, with no internet needed. Unknown merchants stay unclassified instead of being guessed.

Suggests, never decides

A recognised merchant proposes a category while you type. You can change it, and your choice wins.

Merchant spending ranking

See which merchants took the most this month, with their category and how many payments.

Unusual-spend flags, with a high bar

A merchant is flagged only after three payment dates across two months or more, a rise above 50% and at least ₹500 over your usual amount for the same day of the month.

Spots repeating payments

Weekly to yearly patterns need at least three similar payments. Artham prefills the schedule form for you to review, and never creates a schedule or a transaction on its own.

Read-only and local

These insights only read your existing records. No new permission, no external service and no database change.

Local-first

Your data stays on your phone.

What you see₹100.00Hover or tap to flip
What Artham stores10000Whole paise, never fractions of a rupee

Stored on the device

Artham keeps its data in an SQLite database on your phone, managed with Drift.

Exact to the paisa

Amounts are saved as whole paise, so floating-point rounding never creeps into your balances.

Upgrades keep your records

Database migrations are written to preserve existing data. The latest upgrades kept existing transactions and budgets intact.

Alerts, not messages

Imported SMS alerts are saved as structured transaction fields. The original message text is never stored.

Export, locally

Settings can share your transactions as a CSV or save a JSON backup of every table. Files are made on the device and not uploaded. JSON backups are unencrypted, so store them securely.

Database encryption and other security improvements are planned.

ARTHAM

Money should mean something.

Artham is the Malayalam word for meaning. The same word is used for wealth. That is the idea behind the app: know where your money goes, so every rupee has a reason.
